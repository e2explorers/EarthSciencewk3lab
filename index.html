Step 1 — Plot the data

Use the table. For each row, click the graph where the temperature and altitude meet. Adjacent accepted points will connect automatically.

Step 2 — Label the layers

Drag each label into the correct altitude band. The graph only extends to 100 km, so you are labeling the four temperature-defined layers shown by this profile.

Step 3 — Mark the ozone layer

Click the graph at the general altitude where the ozone layer is concentrated. You do not need an exact number.

Step 4 — Review

Look at your completed profile. Where does temperature stop decreasing and start increasing? Where does it reverse again?

Those changes in temperature trend are part of how the major thermal layers of the atmosphere are identified.

Tip: negative temperatures are left of 0 °C. Altitude increases upward.
